# Demonstration and submission standard

The required outputs are a working product, presentation and demo video. Build them from the actual implemented state, not the planned capability catalogue.

## Product handoff

Identify the deployed commit and working public entry point. Show the principal educational scenario end-to-end with meaningful inputs and outputs. Preserve user work through ordinary errors where implemented. Do not claim untested platforms or integrations are complete.

## Presentation

Use concise product language: user problem, implemented workflow, demonstrable benefit, technical decisions relevant to the case and actual limits. Separate measured outcomes from hypotheses. Do not insert fabricated users, accuracy, savings or educational effect.

Use a reproducible slide source, such as the chosen reveal.js path, and inspect every rendered slide. Check text density, clipping, font availability, image provenance and RU/KK/EN content as appropriate. Keep internal setup instructions out of judge-facing materials.

## Demo video

Use an actual functioning flow. Capture at readable scale and verify audio, output playback, duration and transitions. Avoid exposing tokens, internal dashboards with sensitive data, signed URLs or unrelated private tabs. OBS/FFmpeg are tooling choices, not proof the recorded path is live.

Label prerecorded or cached examples honestly. Do not edit the recording to imply a provider response or feature worked when it did not. Keep the final video consistent with the submission's actual capabilities and required format.

## Final evidence

Record what was demonstrated, deployment identity and remaining limitations. Check the published event's actual submission requirements instead of assuming format, deadline or pre-event-code allowances from this preparation pack.
